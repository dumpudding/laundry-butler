LAUNDRY BUTLER — AUTHORITATIVE CONTEXT FOR LLMS
Updated: 2026-08-05

## Purpose

Laundry Butler is a bimanual shirt-folding project using two AgileX Piper
arms, three RGB cameras, ROS 2 Jazzy, and OpenPI pi0.5.

The immediate goal is task-specific performance on one known shirt rather
than broad garment generalization.

## Current task definition

Input state:
- one shirt;
- one size;
- one material;
- shirt begins spread on the table;
- placement is similar between episodes but naturally varies because the
  demonstrations were recorded on real hardware.

Instruction:
- use exactly: "Fold the shirt."

Success:
- the shirt is neatly folded;
- no exact final table position is required;
- arm ending positions may vary;
- both arms should finish lifted away from the shirt.

Dataset exclusions:
- exclude partial folds;
- exclude structurally incomplete recordings;
- exclude episodes marked unusable;
- do not use a "Level 2 messy shirt" initial state;
- the only valid initial-state label is "level_1_spread".

  /piper_right_ctrl_node/arm_status

Do not use:

  /master/joint_left
  /master/joint_right

The authoritative training joint data come from:

  /puppet/joint_left
  /puppet/joint_right

Expected recording rates:
- cameras: approximately 30 Hz;
- arm state: approximately 200 Hz.

Topic presence in rosbag metadata is not enough. Each required topic must
have a nonzero message count and adequate rate.

Before future demonstration recording:
- adjust or lock wrist-camera exposure;
- verify both left and right joint topics;
- verify both left and right end-pose topics;
- verify both arm-status topics;
- keep the emergency stop accessible.

The wrist cameras currently brighten or overexpose as they approach the
black shirt. Do not treat this exposure shift as a reliable distance
signal. It may correlate with distance on this shirt, but it is dependent
on colour, lighting, and image composition.

## Episode timing

Do not trim the beginning of episodes.

The initial pause is intentional:
- camera exposure settles;
- the policy observes the initial shirt configuration;
- the operator identifies the pinch point before contact.

Do not trim episode endings for the current training round.

The retreat and lifted-away terminal pose are part of the demonstrated
task. Automated tail trimming may be considered later only if training
or rollout analysis shows that terminal idle frames cause a clear
problem.

## Joint representation

The physical robot representation is exactly 14 dimensions:

  0  left joint0, radians
  1  left joint1, radians
  2  left joint2, radians
  3  left joint3, radians
  4  left joint4, radians
  5  left joint5, radians
  6  left gripper opening, metres
  7  right joint0, radians
  8  right joint1, radians
  9  right joint2, radians
  10 right joint3, radians
  11 right joint4, radians
  12 right joint5, radians
  13 right gripper opening, metres

Each raw JointState message contains:

  [joint0, joint1, joint2, joint3, joint4, joint5, joint6]

joint6 is the measured gripper opening.

The combined ordering is always:
- left seven values first;
- right seven values second.

Do not silently reorder arms, interleave joints, change signs, or convert
gripper units.

## Dataset conversion

Use:

  raw ROS 2 MCAP
  -> synchronized LeRobot dataset
  -> OpenPI

Do not add an HDF5 intermediate.

The LeRobot dataset must store absolute joint values:
- observation.state: current absolute 14D robot state;
- action: future absolute 14D joint targets before OpenPI transforms.

Images:
- front camera;
- left wrist camera;
- right wrist camera.

Use episode timestamps to align camera and joint streams. Resample the
training/action grid to 30 Hz rather than storing repeated 200 Hz arm
samples as policy steps.

Normalization statistics must be calculated from the selected training
split only.

## OpenPI training decisions

Framework:
- official OpenPI;
- pi0.5;
- JAX training pipeline;
- full fine-tuning.

Do not use:
- LoRA;
- frozen model components;
- Cartesian-relative actions;
- the previous cartrel dataset or transform;
- a robot-specific ALOHA adapter without checking its semantics.

The previous Cartesian-relative pipeline produced unexplained and
incorrect behaviour. Do not reintroduce cartrel merely because older
scripts or datasets still exist.

Action representation:
- store absolute 14D actions in LeRobot;
- enable relative-joint actions inside OpenPI for the 12 arm joints;
- keep both grippers absolute.

Relative-action mask:

  indices 0-5:   relative
  index 6:       absolute gripper
  indices 7-12:  relative
  index 13:      absolute gripper

Conceptually:

  relative_arm_action =
      future_absolute_arm_position - current_arm_position

At inference, OpenPI must convert predicted relative arm actions back to
absolute joint targets before sending them to Piper.

Do not precompute relative actions in LeRobot and then apply the OpenPI
relative-action transform again.

Create a Piper-specific OpenPI data/policy configuration. The robot has
the same physical action dimension as ALOHA, but a 14D shape match does
not prove that ALOHA-specific joint or gripper adaptation is correct.

## Training scale

Training hardware:
- eight NVIDIA A800 GPUs.

Do not increase batch size simply because eight GPUs are available.

Initial global batch:
  32

Initial distribution:
  4 samples per GPU across eight GPUs

Increase to 64 only if measurements show a concrete stability or
throughput benefit.

No frozen components.
No LoRA.

The dataset is highly homogeneous, so evaluate multiple checkpoints
instead of assuming that the lowest training loss is best.

The intent is to train far enough to overfit the target task while
testing where physical rollout performance peaks.

## Action timing and inference

Training/action frequency:
  30 Hz

Predicted action horizon:
  50 steps

At 30 Hz:
  50 steps = approximately 1.67 seconds

Initial execution horizon:
  execute 20 predicted steps, then replan

Comparison:
  test executing 30 predicted steps before replanning

At 30 Hz:
  20 steps = approximately 0.67 seconds
  30 steps = approximately 1.00 second

Do not execute all 50 steps open-loop for the first tests.

No explicit command interpolation or smoothing is required for the first
rollout.

Send the 30 Hz absolute targets produced after the inverse relative-action
transform, provided the Piper command interface accepts this safely.
Inspect whether the Piper driver already performs internal interpolation
before adding another smoothing layer.

Smooth appearance is secondary to task success. Do not add smoothing if
it weakens cloth-contact timing or corrective motion.

## Evaluation

Initial evaluation uses the same shirt represented in training.

Evaluate several checkpoints physically. At minimum, compare:
- an early checkpoint;
- a middle checkpoint;
- a later, deliberately overfit checkpoint.

Record:
- whether the shirt was neatly folded;
- failed or missed pinch points;
- failed grasps;
- cloth alignment errors;
- premature stopping;
- unsafe or out-of-range commands;
- whether both arms retreat clear of the folded shirt.

No exact final shirt location or arm pose is required.

## Safety and inference requirements

Before sending policy outputs:
- restore relative arm outputs to absolute joint targets;
- preserve absolute gripper outputs;
- enforce Piper joint limits;
- enforce gripper limits;
- reject NaN or infinite actions;
- limit unsafe command discontinuities;
- provide an immediate stop mechanism.

Playback and dataset review must never publish onto live robot command
topics.

## Known failure points

1. Cartesian-relative actions previously produced unexplained behaviour.
   Do not use cartrel for this round.

2. A previous dataset/checkpoint path mixed 16D Cartesian data with a
   14D robot representation. Confirm 14D semantics, not only tensor
   shapes.

3. A rosbag can contain a topic definition while containing zero messages
   for that topic. Check message counts.

4. One confirmed episode recorded only the right arm and was excluded:
   episode_20260804_154016_021_shirt-folding.

5. Wrist-camera auto-exposure can hide shirt edges near contact.

6. ALOHA and Piper both use 14 physical dimensions, but their adapters
   must not be assumed interchangeable.

7. The OpenPI source and configuration used on the DGX are authoritative.
   A stale or extracted OpenPI copy on the recording workstation is not.
